When talking about commercial air curtains, successful performance depends on matching airflow, dimensions, heating type and installation configuration to the actual building. A commercial air curtain should create a continuous air stream across the entrance rather than simply provide warm air close to the doorway.

A commercial air curtain installed at the hotel entrance creates a continuous air barrier whenever required. Heated commercial air curtains can reduce cold draughts and support comfortable lobby temperatures, while ambient commercial air curtains can provide environmental separation during warmer periods.

The control arrangement can depend on the specific commercial premises. Some commercial air curtains may operate according to doorway use, while other installations may require continuous operation during trading hours because the entrance remains permanently open.

Because the heating takes place directly within the commercial air curtain, electric models provide a straightforward method of delivering heated airflow. The electrical capacity of the premises and the required heat output should be considered when selecting the appropriate commercial air curtain.

Maintenance also affects long-term commercial air curtain performance. Air inlets need to remain clear so the fans can draw sufficient air through the unit. Dust accumulation can reduce airflow and place unnecessary demand on components.

If a commercial air curtain is positioned too far away from the opening, the air stream may not form the intended barrier. The same principle applies to vertically installed commercial air curtains, which should be positioned appropriately alongside the entrance.

Commercial air curtains can also be mounted vertically. Vertical installation can be particularly useful for wider entrances, larger commercial openings or situations where there is insufficient mounting space above the doorway. When commercial air curtains are positioned vertically on both sides of an entrance, door widths of up to approximately 8 metres can be accommodated. When installed vertically on one side, a commercial air curtain can accommodate entrances up to approximately 4 metres wide, subject to the individual installation conditions.

During warmer conditions, commercial air curtains can support air-conditioned retail environments. Instead of retaining heat, the commercial air curtain helps retain cooled indoor air and limits the movement of warmer external air into the shop.

During winter, heated air leaving an open shop entrance represents energy that has already been produced by the building's heating system. Cold air entering through the same doorway can create draughts and reduce comfort around tills, displays and other areas close to the entrance.

Retail shops are a common location for commercial air curtains because customer access and energy management often need to be balanced. An open shop door can make a retail premises easily accessible, but it also allows indoor air to escape continuously.

The heating output of water-heated commercial air curtains depends on the flow and return temperatures of the water supplied to the heat exchanger. Higher water temperatures can provide greater available heating output, while lower-temperature systems require appropriate commercial air curtain selection according to the desired performance.

Maintaining this separation can have important implications for commercial heating and cooling. During colder weather, an open entrance can allow heated indoor air to escape while colder external air enters the building. The heating system then has to compensate for the additional heat loss. Commercial air curtains help reduce this exchange and can support lower heating energy requirements associated with frequently opened entrances.

Horizontal and vertical installation options allow commercial air curtains to be adapted to different building layouts. A conventional retail doorway may be suitable for a commercial air curtain installed directly above the entrance, while a wide commercial doorway may be better served by vertical commercial air curtains installed on either side.

Vertical commercial air curtains extend the possible applications to wider entrances, with arrangements capable of covering openings up to approximately 8 metres when units are installed on both sides.

Modern commercial air curtains are designed to balance airflow performance with controlled sound power levels. Selecting the correct commercial air curtain rather than operating an undersized unit continuously at maximum output can also contribute to more appropriate operating conditions.

For restaurants operating open-door dining or trading, commercial air curtains can be particularly useful. An air curtain can maintain a degree of environmental separation even when the physical entrance remains open for extended periods.

External wind conditions should also be considered. A commercial entrance located in a sheltered shopping centre experiences different conditions from an exposed doorway facing prevailing winds. Greater external pressure can require stronger commercial air curtain airflow.

The objective is to match commercial air curtain operation with actual building requirements. Providing unnecessary heating increases energy consumption, while insufficient airflow reduces the effectiveness of the air barrier.
